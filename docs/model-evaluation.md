# Model evaluation

2026-10-17, local Brave WebGPU. These are authored Czech/English development cases, not independent accuracy estimates.

## Selected: Laya multilingual FP16 (322M)

The final default combines explicit topic phrases with Laya's scores for five natural-language conditions. At a 0.45 threshold it passed 43/44 cases, including 19/20 held-out cases. The threshold was chosen on the first 24 cases and left unchanged for the final 20. There were no false hides in this small set. The missed case was hostile US election coverage: its score was 0.433, below the threshold. These results measure the combined default, not the model alone.

Median model inference was 112.2 ms per post on this machine, after loading. The benchmark runs inference even for phrase matches; production skips inference for those matches. The packaged model needs roughly 647 MB of ONNX graph/weights plus a 34 MB tokenizer, and requires WebGPU with shader-f16 support.

Raw local results: [evaluation.json](../artifacts/evaluation.json). The report is generated by `bun run lab`; artifacts are ignored by Git.

A synthetic scrolling fixture also passed: three excluded posts were removed below the fold while five ordinary Czech posts remained, without visible blank gaps.

## Actual X feed scrolling

On 2026-10-17 the unpacked extension was installed and activated in the user's signed-in Brave profile. X was reloaded and scrolled down eight screenfuls, including two-screen jumps. DOM snapshots covered 31 distinct posts; eight distinct posts were observed hidden at some point. No pending rows remained at the final inspection, no warning/error console messages were captured, and screenshots showed adjacent posts without obvious empty gaps. This is a short integration smoke test, not a speed or accuracy measurement.

The defaults are not ready to trust unattended. A software-job history post was incorrectly hidden; running the same visible text in the local lab reproduced a 0.544 score. Foreign riot/outrage coverage that remained visible scored only 0.133 in the lab. A single threshold adjustment cannot correct both examples. The live sample is therefore stronger evidence against the current semantic defaults than the authored benchmark is evidence for them.

Some posts were already visible at startup and are deliberately left unchanged; other misses need further investigation of the active settings and timing. One Czech outrage post scored 0.468 in a separate lab run, above the fresh-install threshold of 0.45, so its visible state alone does not establish a model miss. Image-only context and text hidden by X's Show more control were not classified.

The test also caught a previously hidden post being recreated above the viewport by X and losing its hidden state. The local fix remembers up to 500 decisions and Show post overrides by post ID plus text, clears them on settings changes, and preserves a placeholder if a known rejection is recreated in view. Three regression tests cover recreated rows, Show post, and changed settings. All 19 tests and native TypeScript checking pass. The rebuilt extension has not been reloaded for live verification: browser automation blocks the extensions page and alternate routes around that restriction.

## Decision Lab V3 INT4 (350M)

Rejected: 12/24 correct at a 0.8 hide threshold, median 86.9 ms, first request 1225.9 ms. Most ordinary and excluded posts scored similarly (roughly 0.59–0.79), so changing the threshold would not fix the weak separation. The prompt was the initial prose form of the default preference.

## Multilingual MiniLM L6 NLI (107M)

Rejected: 10/24 correct at 0.8 on the same smoke cases (natural-language conditions on separate lines), median 13.5 ms. Entailment-vs-contradiction scoring was overconfident on unrelated Czech posts; neutral evidence is an important limitation of this readout.
